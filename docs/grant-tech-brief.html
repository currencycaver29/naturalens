The pitch

Most people who see an animal in the wild cannot name it — and naming is the first step toward caring about it. Field guides are slow. Experts are rare. Other apps often want an account, a community, or a live internet connection in places that do not have one.

NaturaLens is a personal field notebook: take a photo, get a name in about two seconds, save the find, see it later on a list and on a map. No account. No social feed. That loop already works. The website is live. People can join a waitlist. We can invite testers on Apple’s TestFlight. In parallel we are photographing wildlife ourselves — starting with polar bears — so the same app can later name animals on the phone, even with no signal.

Why a grant should care

Conservation only works if the tool gets used

You cannot protect what you cannot name

Education and conservation both start with recognition. If naming an animal takes a guidebook or a good data connection, most people never do it. We are building the fastest honest loop we can: look, name, keep.

The places that need this have the worst signal

A wildlife tool that dies without cell service is a compromised field tool. Cloud naming lets us ship now and learn from real use. Teaching our own small model is how it later works offline, cheaper, and more privately — which is the version that belongs in the field.

If they ask how the naming works

Two sentences. Then stop.

Today the photo goes to a cloud service, which sends back a name and a few facts. Next, a small model we taught on our own photos will run on the phone, with the cloud as backup when it is unsure.

We did not invent a new kind of AI. We rent a fast naming service so real people can use the product now, and we teach a compact, already-trained model on our field photos so we are not rented forever. Polar bear is first because we can collect and label it, it is visually distinct, and the conservation story is real. More species come after the loop is proven. The name is a helpful guess, not a scientific ID — we would rather say “we’re not sure” than invent an answer.

Questions they actually ask

Answers in plain language

Why not just use iNaturalist or Seek?

Those products prove people want this. We are earlier and simpler: a private log, our own field photos, and a path to working without the internet. We are not claiming we beat their models today. We are building the loop we need in order to own the naming instead of renting it forever.

Where do the photos go? Is this private?

For naming, the picture is sent out for that one request. The saved log stays on the phone. No accounts. No public map of other people’s finds. Moving naming onto the phone is how privacy and offline arrive together.

Is it on the App Store? What is actually novel?

Not publicly. TestFlight is a closed invite — real software on real phones, not a slide deck. Do not oversell a new invention. The work that is ours is the closed loop: a product people can use, photos we took ourselves, and a path onto the phone. Grants fund that execution and that data.

Money

Ask for a small operating budget. Not a supercomputer.

A defensible band is about $2,000–$8,000 for a few months, or the grant’s mini-award. Apple’s developer fee is already paid. Cloud naming is a fraction of a cent per photo; on-phone naming is pay-once — rent it long enough to learn, then stop renting.

The money is for

  • Naming-service usage, so testers are not blocked when the free allowance runs out.
  • Time in the field taking photos — our dataset, not stock pictures.

The money is not for

  • A computer cluster, or “training AI from scratch.”
  • A photo library, a labeling farm, accounts, or a social network.
Stay honest · then the one paragraph

Do not reach. Then say this.

Do not say: we built our own ChatGPT · naming already happens on the phone · we are on the App Store · we store everyone’s sightings on our servers · photos never leave the phone · we bought a photo library · we have thousands of labeled images · the name is scientifically verified.

We already have a working app that names an animal from a photo and keeps a private log. The grant is not to invent a new kind of AI — it is to run that loop, grow our field photos, and teach a small model so it works with no signal. Polar bears first, then more species.
